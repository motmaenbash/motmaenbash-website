Phishing basics

Phishing is a scam that pretends to be a real organization or service and tries to steal identity details, bank information, or passwords.

  • The attacker pretends to be a trusted organization
  • The message arrives by email, SMS, or a social network
  • The page is designed to look like a real payment gateway or a known service
  • The person is pushed to enter private information
A common example: a fake SMS about a court notice. The link leads to a page that asks you to install a malicious app or enter bank details.
Attackers use several tricks:
  • Texts promising a prize or free mobile data.
  • Fake bank payment pages that collect card details.
  • Malicious apps shared in social networks and messengers.
  • Fake emails with urgent warnings, such as “your account is blocked” or “confirm your information now”.
Information entered on those pages goes to the attacker.
Common phishing:
  • Fake text messages
  • Fake bank pages
  • Malicious apps
  • Urgent emails
Bait texts
  • Free credit
  • Fake bank warnings
  • Suspicious short links
Fake apps
  • Copies of banking apps
  • Copies of government apps
  • Unknown VPN tools

Look for these signs:
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes
  • Shortened or unusual links
  • Bait such as “you have won a prize” or “you have an unpaid toll”
  • Claims about a subsidy payment, or that you were removed from a payment list
  • A message pretending to be the judiciary and asking you to open a notice or a case
  • A personal phone number instead of an official sender such as adliran
  • A request for personal information, a card password, or a confirmation code
  • A threat such as “your account will be blocked if you do not pay”

Look for these signs:
  • A sender domain that only looks real, such as support@google-support.com instead of @google.com
  • Shortened or unusual links
  • A link domain that only looks real, such as inistagram.com instead of instagram.com
  • A request for private information after you open a link, such as a username and password
  • Urgent threats, such as losing access to the account
  • An attachment you were not expecting

These habits help:
  • Learn the current tricks, and warn people around you
  • Look at the site carefully before entering information
  • Install mobile apps only from official stores
  • Pay attention to warnings from the browser and other security tools
  • Do not enter information on a site you do not know
  • Delete suspicious texts and emails
  • Do not open unexpected links in messengers, social networks, SMS, or email
  • Do not install unknown apps, including VPNs, from Telegram or other messengers

How MotmaenBash works

The Android app uses the checks below. 🚀

SMS
  • Checks new SMS messages and flags suspicious ones
  • Scans links in those messages and warns you
  • Shows a warning when a message looks suspicious
Browser
  • Checks links opened in the browser
  • Shows a green confirmation on genuine Shaparak payment pages
  • Warns you immediately about a suspicious or misleading site
Apps
  • Warns you when a suspicious app is installed
  • Can scan apps that are already installed
  • Warns you about apps that ask for suspicious permissions
General
  • Shows short security tips on the home screen.
  • Updates the database as new threats appear.
  • Works offline, at the moment the message, link, or app is seen.
  • Lets people report new scams, and those reports improve the database.

No. The app does not upload user information to a server, it works offline, and it has no central server. Suspicious links, phishing SMS, and malicious apps are checked on the device. The on-device database is updated from a GitHub repository, and that update does not send anything about you.

The browser extension does two things:
  • Checks the address of payment gateways
  • Shows a green mark when the payment gateway looks genuine

Use the report form for phishing SMS messages, sites, and apps. Reports help MotmaenBash recognize more cases.
